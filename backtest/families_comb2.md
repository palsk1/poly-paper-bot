What it says, in plain words:

- **Buying Yes loses everywhere**: about $21 per $100 over 10,000 bets, in every family, in both halves. People overpay for "it will happen".
- **Buying No does not win it back**: about -$5 per $100. The gap between buyers and sellers eats the lean.
- **The Rotten Tomatoes lean does not travel.** No on "probably yes" early in a market's life is ahead in the first half of the bets and behind in the second, in almost every family. The boxes that look good for No on near-sure things (Yes at 75-97c in commodities and music) are a handful of No tickets bought for 4-12c that came in.
- **No on long shots loses a little** when bought at any time of a market's life (the stock track only buys in the last afternoon, which is a different bet, and still unproven).
