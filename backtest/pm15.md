# The kc_maker_safe bid on Polymarket's 15-minute crypto windows (12 Nov 2026, Jan's ask)

Jan: "why is this test only running on kalshi and not both kalshi and polymarket?" So I replayed it there.
Research only, paper only. Code: `kcrypto.py pmfetch` / `pmrun` (probe `kc_pmall_30`, run 43271610871).

## In plain words

- **On Polymarket this bid does not work.** Same rule as kc_maker_safe (side at 95c+, a few minutes left, Binance
  says the coin is 2+ typical moves past the line, wait with a buy order, hold to the end), 31 days:
  **-$1.43 per $100** with 3 minutes left (363 bets, 13 lost). With 2 or 4 minutes left it's about even
  (+$0.27 and +$0.10). The exam says "Behind, but could be bad luck"; counting only the surest fills, "Losing".
- **It loses about 4 times as often as on Kalshi.** Polymarket: 1 loss in 28 bets (1 in 44-54 at 2 or 4 minutes).
  Kalshi on the same 31 days: 1 loss in 187 bets, +$0.65 per $100 (4,109 bets). At these prices you need fewer
  than 1 loss in about 45 just to break even.
- **The Binance price-gap fix doesn't rescue it:** -$1.26 per $100 at 3 minutes (158 bets, 4 lost).
- **There are far fewer bets.** About 12 a day on Polymarket against about 130 on Kalshi. Outside bitcoin and
  ether these Polymarket windows barely trade in the last minutes (typically 10-40 trades in 8 minutes).
- Likely reasons (my reading, not proven): Polymarket decides on one single price at the last second, Kalshi on
  the average of the last minute, so a late wobble flips Polymarket more easily. And with so little trading, the
  people who do sell into a waiting bid late are mostly the ones who see the coin turning.

**Verdict: dropped for Polymarket, no track.** Kalshi stays the home of this strategy.

## How it was tested, and what could be off

- Polymarket has no saved history of buy and sell offers for these windows (our recorder caught only about 400
  of them late enough). So the replay uses **every real trade** of the last 8 minutes of 18,600 windows.
- Our price = the lowest price the leading side really traded at in the 15 seconds before we bid (one tick higher
  when there was room). Filled only when a later real trade happened at or below it; the "surest fills" row
  needs a trade strictly below it.
- The line: Polymarket doesn't publish its starting price in the data, so I used Kalshi's line for the same
  window (both are US-dollar prices). Wins and losses are Polymarket's own official results. The two sites
  disagreed on 4 of 1,867 signals.
- Not tested: a live paper run reading Polymarket's real offers. Given 4 times the loss rate and a tenth of the
  bets, I don't think it's worth building.
